## 1. Maps: 3D Map Route
A clean 3D city in the style of a phone maps app. The camera lifts off from behind a blue location dot while the route draws itself through the streets, a red pin drops on the destination and the trip card pops up from the bottom. It turns "here's where it is" into one satisfying shot. Use it for travel recaps, "where we went" reels, store or venue locations, delivery and logistics explainers.

### The look
- Palette: soft grey-white city (buildings #F7F7F5, taller ones #EEF0F3, land #ECEAE4, streets #FBFBFA), pale haze #E4E9EE, water #8EC6EE, parks #C9E5BD with round trees #9FCF8E, one pale yellow main road #FFE3A3. The route is blue #1A73E8 on a white casing. The pin is red #EA4335 with a white rim. The trip card is white with near-black #121417 type, grey #6B737C details, a green #1F9D55 "Fastest route" and a blue Go button.
- Type: Inter throughout. Place labels in SemiBold grey with a soft white halo. The destination label in Bold red. On the card, the minutes in ExtraBold with tight tracking and fixed-width numbers, then a small Medium line under it.
- Layout: the city fills the frame at a high three-quarter angle once settled. The route runs from the bottom left up to the pin in the top right, crossing the river. Three place labels sit in open ground, never on the route. The trip card is a wide rounded sheet just above the bottom fifth, which stays calm.
- Texture: plain matte blocks under one soft sun, so every building casts a soft shadow, with a gentle darkening where walls meet the ground. Distance fades into haze, and a pale sky shows only while the camera is low.

### How it moves
- Frame one is already the city: a low, close view behind the blue dot, looking up the street, with a short stub of route ahead and a soft pulse leaving the dot.
- The camera rises and backs away first, then pans and narrows onto the destination, landing on the high view with a long, soft ease. No orbiting, no wobble. The dot and the route keep a near-constant size on screen, the way a maps app draws them.
- The route draws along the streets, turning the corners with rounded joins, and slows as it reaches the destination.
- The pin falls in, squashes slightly on impact and settles; its label snaps in with a small overshoot. The trip card springs up from below with about 10% overshoot, and the minutes roll down 14, 13, 12 like an odometer. Then everything holds for the last second.

### Never
- Real brands, real cities or real map data. Invented streets and place names only.
- Spin the camera round the city or bounce it. Only the pin, labels and card overshoot.
- Fade labels or the card on. They pop, drop or slide.
- Dark mode, neon, glowing routes or a busy colour palette. The city stays pale so the blue route and red pin do the talking.
